During my internship, I worked on two software projects that addressed different problems through the practical use of artificial intelligence and backend application development. The first project, SecureRAG, focused on making information stored in documents easier to find and understand. The second project, TenderAI, focused on identifying relevant construction tenders and helping users assess whether those opportunities were suitable for further consideration. Although the projects served different purposes, both involved processing large amounts of information and presenting useful results through an application.
SecureRAG was developed to help users ask questions about their documents and receive answers supported by retrieved information. The system brings together document extraction, optical character recognition (OCR), text processing, semantic search, keyword-based retrieval, and large language models. Its retrieval pipeline combines vector search with BM25 keyword matching, followed by a reranking stage to improve the selection of relevant content. Security-related mechanisms, including input filtering, rate limiting, personally identifiable information masking, and audit logging, were also incorporated. The project includes a React-based interface and a Tauri desktop application shell, allowing the document-questioning workflow to be accessed through a desktop environment.
TenderAI addresses a different challenge: finding useful procurement opportunities among tender notices published through public portals. Its workflow includes tender collection, document text extraction, construction-relevance classification, suitability scoring, and the presentation of results through a web dashboard. A rule-based classification module assigns categories and recommendations such as GO, REVIEW, and NO-GO based on defined patterns and rejection rules. The system also includes language-model integration for selected text-generation tasks, database storage, and PDF export functionality. During verification, sample construction and non-construction notices produced different classification outcomes, demonstrating how domain-specific rules can help filter irrelevant opportunities. The available project database also contained 11 tender records that were inspected during verification.
This report describes the objectives, design, implementation, and verification of both projects. It discusses the technologies used, the workflows followed, the observed results, and the limitations identified during development. Together, the projects provided practical experience in document processing, information retrieval, API development, database integration, AI-assisted workflows, application interfaces, and software testing. The report also identifies improvements that could make the systems more reliable and suitable for broader use.
Keywords: Artificial Intelligence, Retrieval-Augmented Generation, SecureRAG, TenderAI, Optical Character Recognition, Hybrid Search, Large Language Models, Tender Classification, Document Processing, FastAPI.
